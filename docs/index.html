Mynah

A private voice appliance. It runs on a Mac you own. You reach it by messaging yourself on Signal, and you can call it and talk.

What it is

Mynah is an agent that lives on one Mac. It remembers what you have told it, it can search the web, write notes and keep a backlog, and it answers in the same Signal thread you wrote to.

It is not a service. There is no account and nothing to sign into. The Mac it runs on is yours, the Signal account is yours, and the model that answers is either running on that same Mac or on an API key you supply.

What you need

  • A Mac, left switched on Apple Silicon, macOS 14 or later. A Mac mini is the usual choice. It has to be awake to answer, so a laptop that sleeps in a bag is the wrong machine for it.
  • Signal, on your phone Mynah links to your existing account as a secondary device — the same way Signal Desktop does. Your number does not change, your phone stays the primary device, and you do not register anything new.
  • A model Either one running on the Mac itself, which the app can install for you, or an API key from a provider you choose. The setup screen tells you, for each option, where your words end up. Messages and voice notes work with either. Calling needs the API one, for a reason worth reading under Calling it.

Calling needs one further thing — a small relay you run — also described under Calling it.

Messaging it

Open Signal and message yourself. Note to Self. Mynah reads that thread and answers in it, so the whole conversation sits in the one place you already keep things you write to yourself.

Text works. Voice notes work — they are transcribed on the Mac. Photos work; send one with no caption and it will tell you what it sees. If you send three messages in quick succession it waits a moment and answers the thought rather than each fragment.

Slow answers say so. If it has gone to look something up, you get a line telling you that, and then another only if there is something new to report. Silence past a certain point is a failure, and the appliance is built to not be silent.

Every reply is marked with a small label. Signal draws Note to Self as a single column of your own messages, so without one there is no way to see at a glance which lines are yours.

Two things you can type

Anything else you send is just a question. These two are instructions to the program, and it answers them itself rather than passing them to the model.

//help Lists what you can say, and tells you whether calling will work on the model you are running. Also answers to //commands and //?.
//call Sets up a voice call and sends back a link.

If you remember one thing, make it //help. You are in a message thread and not reading a manual, and a command nobody has mentioned to you does not exist as far as you are concerned — so that is the one that goes and finds the others.

Both have to be the whole message. Writing how do I use //call is a question and gets answered as one, not treated as an instruction to open a microphone on your phone.

Calling it

Send //call and you get a link back. Tap it, allow the microphone, and you are talking to it. There is nothing to install: the link opens a web page, and the page is the whole client.

It is a real conversation. You can cut it off mid-sentence and it stops — not at the end of its answer, within a fraction of a second. An assistant you cannot interrupt is just a slower voice note.

What happens when you tap the link

  1. The page asks your browser for the microphone. The browser is doing real work here: it cancels the echo of Mynah's own voice coming back through your speaker, suppresses room noise, and levels your volume. Without the first of those, full duplex is impossible — the appliance hears itself and talks over its own answer.
  2. Your phone and the Mac describe to each other how they can be reached. That description goes through a relay, because it has to go somewhere both of them can see.
  3. They connect directly. On your home network that is your phone and the Mac across the room, and nothing in between. On a network where a direct path cannot be found, the audio is relayed instead — still sealed, and still unreadable to the relay.
  4. You talk. It waits until you have actually finished a sentence rather than cutting in on the pause before you name something, and it starts speaking its answer as soon as the first sentence of it is ready.

Calling needs an API model. A model running on the Mac itself takes the better part of a minute to begin answering, because it is sharing the machine with everything else Mynah is doing. In a message thread that is a wait, and Mynah is built to make waiting bearable — it tells you it has gone to look, and tells you again if it is taking a while. In a conversation none of that helps. You would be listening to silence on an open line, and a caller will not sit through a pause a reader barely notices.

So Mynah refuses the call rather than letting you discover this halfway through one. It tells you which model is running, why it will not do, and that changing the model in its settings fixes it. Messages and voice notes keep working on either kind, and it says that too.

Calling also needs a relay you run. It is a small program on a rented host with a real domain name. The reason is unglamorous: a Mac on a home network has no name a certificate authority will sign, and a browser that distrusts the page it is holding will quietly refuse to connect the call. So the page is served from somewhere with a proper certificate, and the Mac dials out to it. Nothing is forwarded to your home network and no port is opened.

Where your words go

This is the product's whole argument, so it is worth being exact about it rather than reassuring.

Signal messages stays
End-to-end encrypted, between your own devices, in your own Note to Self thread. Mynah answers only there. Messages from anyone else, and messages in groups, are refused before anything reads them.
Voice notes and speech recognition stays
Transcribed on your Mac. The audio is not uploaded anywhere, and the transcriber refuses to send it anywhere but the local machine.
The voice you hear stays
Synthesised on your Mac.
Notes and memory stays
Written to disk on your Mac, readable only by your account. Mynah can also pass something to another agent if you ask it to — but only if you have connected one, and only when you ask.
The model depends
If you chose a local model, your words stay on the Mac. If you chose an API, your words go to that provider and are handled under their terms. That is a real difference and the setup screen states it per option rather than leaving you to infer it.
Web searches leaves
When it looks something up, the search terms go to a search engine, as searches do.
Call audio, on a direct path stays
Usually there is one. Your phone and the Mac work out how to reach each other, and on a home network that is the two of them across the room. The audio goes straight between them and never touches the relay.
Call audio, when there is no direct path leaves
Some networks — mobile networks especially — allow no direct route at all. The audio is then carried by a separate relay server, so the packets do cross a rented machine. They stay sealed end to end the whole way and cannot be listened to there. Running that server yourself is what keeps even the fact of it in your hands.
That a call happened leaves
The relay sets the call up, so it necessarily learns that a call occurred, when, and between which network addresses — that is what the setup messages contain. It cannot hear the call. This is the honest cost of calls working at all, and it is why the relay is a program you run on a host you rent rather than a service you are asked to trust.

What it is not

It does not listen. Nothing is recorded and nothing is transcribed until you send it something. There is no wake word and no microphone open on the Mac.

It does not start conversations. It answers the thread you wrote to, and that is the only thing it does unprompted — nothing.

It is not always right. It is a language model with tools, and it can misread a voice note, misjudge a search result, or be confidently wrong. Everything it did during a turn ends up in the reply you read, which is the point: you are the one checking it.

It is not finished. It works, it is in daily use, and there are rough edges. If something breaks, say so — the logs on the Mac usually know exactly where it stopped.
